I hadn't played VR since my Rift S broke. Which, honestly, was not a huge loss. The Rift S was such a shit product. So when the Steam Frame was announced I was hyped (ai am a valve shill.)

Then came the reservation roulette. I was fully manifesting. And somehow I actually got on the reservation list and not the waitlist?? Meanwhile I'm watching youtubers sitting on the waitlist like oops I bought mine already?

I got the 1TB model plus the ergo kit, which came out to about $1.2k. Of course my headset got incorrectly sorted in shipping while my accessories showed up right on time. fml. All I wanted was to wake and bake and get on VRChat at 7am,,,,, is that so much to ask ToT

## Unboxing

First thing: I am in shock and awe at the size of this thing. It's SO TINY.

The box itself is like your average tech box nowadays. Everything fits nicely and it feels relatively premium. Setup was more confusing than it needed to be though. It could really use a better instruction manual of sorts, I didnt like having to watch videos to figure it out.

The controllers fit a bit strange in my hands. The thumbsticks are a little hard to reach, but they seem pretty standard for VR controllers. They also get sweaty pretty easily.

## Linux: rough at launch

I use Linux on my desktop, so naturally that was the first thing I tried. And sadly, the Linux experience at launch is bad. Playing games on the headset itself is great, but I couldnt really use the PC game streaming.

First, SteamVR wouldnt start when I launched a game from the headset, so games just wouldnt launch in VR mode. I forced VR mode on launch from the terminal lmfao. And then I got to experience the frame skipping.......

Everything I ran into was already a reported, known issue. I checked GitHub: Valve said they'd fix [SteamVR not starting](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/SteamVR-for-Linux/issues/962), but [the frame dropping](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/SteamVR-for-Linux/issues/965), which is the more important problem, is still open. The stream freezes for a second or two every so often, while the same hardware works fine on Windows. As far as I can tell nothing has really changed on the Linux side since launch. Some people in that thread are trying driver patches, but it's not a real fix yet.

## Windows streaming

So I went back to Windows, which I hadnt touched in months. Now I only boot it for VR I stream from my laptop (sealtop, he has a 5070 Ti & 32G of DDR5, but tends to max out to thermal throttle instead...)

It's a lot better, actually playable, but still not what I expected. Whenever there's a big performance drop the stream cuts out for a few seconds and you just cant see the game, which is mid.

## Battery & refresh rate

Unfortunately I notice the refresh rate difference and cant stand the default 90Hz. 120Hz looks great but sucks the power down quick. Streaming at 120, it died in about 2 hours.

Honestly, the only reason I stream at all is because the battery life is shite. I'm hoping someone makes a battery pack peripheral (the Frame is literally built for peripherals) so I can just play on the onboard hardware. Until then I need a 20ft USB-C cable or something so I can play plugged in. I havent gotten one yet lol.

Even plugged in it wouldnt be that bad though, since it's just 1 USB-C cable instead of 2 big heavy ones running down your back.

So far I've been playing VRChat and Beat Saber.

## What I love

**The weight.** I used to play VR for an hour or two and then my neck would hurt because the headset was too damn heavy. With the Steam Frame I barely even notice it's there. It's less powerful than some headsets, I know, but I'll take that trade.

**The modding.** It was built to accommodate modules. If you want to completely replace the whole chassis you can: it's all magnetic and so easy to take apart.

**No Meta.** Being freed from Meta's data stealing shackles is really nice.

## "But it's so expensive"

Rant time. Meta doesnt actually make a profit on VR. Reality Labs, their VR division, [lost about $19 billion in 2025 alone](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/yes-reality-labs-is-still-losing-billions). But they keep making more VR products, because to use any of their headsets you have to sell your soul and every data point inside it to them. They make so much money off your data that they can keep pouring it back into VR... so they can harvest more data...

Part of the reason the Steam Frame looks so "expensive" is that the headsets it gets compared to are the Meta ones. Which hardly seems fair once you take their business strategy into account.

Computers are super expensive right now. So I dont really get people complaining about the price. I think it's very fair for the value you get.

## 3 weeks later

I'm still using it, mainly for VRChat. Nothing new on the Linux side that I know of.

Would I recommend it? Yes, if you already play VR and enjoy it. But if you dont find yourself using VR a lot, it's a pretty pricey toy, so pick wisely.
